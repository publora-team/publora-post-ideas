# Voice

The point of this skill is a post the user would have written. That is a higher
bar than a post that is correct, and it fails in ways that are easy to miss and
embarrassing to publish.

## Read the voice, do not ask for it

Asking someone to describe their own writing voice produces adjectives:
"friendly but professional", "authentic". Adjectives are useless here. The voice
is in the mechanics, and the mechanics are visible in what they have already
posted.

With Publora connected, `list_posts` gives you the material. Read ten or fifteen
real posts, ignoring test runs, and read for these specifics:

- **Person.** "I" or "we"? Many founders switch: "we" for product news, "I" for
  anything that cost them something. Match the switch, do not flatten it.
- **Paragraph length.** One-sentence paragraphs, or three to four lines? This is
  the most visible property of a post and the easiest to get wrong.
- **Sentence length variance.** Uniform medium-length sentences are the clearest
  machine tell. Real writing alternates. Look at whether they use short
  fragments, and how often.
- **Openings.** Do they start with a scene, a number, a claim, or a question?
  People are consistent about this without noticing.
- **Endings.** A question to the audience, a flat statement, a link, nothing?
- **Punctuation habits.** Em dashes, colons, ellipses, parentheses. Someone who
  never uses a semicolon should not start now.
- **Emoji and hashtags.** Count them. Zero is a style. Match it exactly; adding
  two friendly emoji to someone who uses none is the single most obvious tell.
- **Register.** Do they write "do not" or "don't"? Contractions are a strong
  signal and models drift formal.

## What to carry over and what not to

Carry the mechanics. Do not carry the sentences.

If the post is built on something longer the user already wrote — an article, a
changelog, release notes — the temptation is to lift its best lines, because
they are already good. Do not. The same phrasing landing on two channels within
a few days reads as repackaging, and the people who follow both will notice.
Take the fact, write it new, and link to the original.

## Vocabulary that is not theirs

Some words almost never appear in real posts and appear constantly in generated
ones. If a word is in the draft and not in fifteen of the user's posts, it is
probably yours, not theirs: *delve, leverage, robust, seamless, empower,
landscape, realm, testament, crucial, pivotal, elevate, unlock, harness,
navigate the complexities, it's not just X, it's Y.*

The same applies to structures: the rule of three, the two-part contrast
("not X, but Y"), the closing one-liner that reverses the opening. These are not
wrong in isolation. They are wrong three times in one short post.

## When there is nothing to read

New account, empty history, or no Publora connection: say so rather than
inventing a voice. Ask for one thing instead of guessing — a link to a post they
liked writing, or two sentences typed the way they would say them out loud — and
work from that.

## The check before showing

Read the draft aloud. Every skill file can say the same thing, but this is the
test that catches what the others miss: if you would not say this sentence to a
person, the user will not say it to their audience either.
