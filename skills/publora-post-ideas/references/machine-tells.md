# Machine tells

A draft can pass every automated check and still read as written by a model.
Scripts catch vocabulary and structure. This file is for what they miss, and it
is the last pass before showing the user anything.

Each entry below is a real pattern, not a style preference. The fix is the same
in every case: say the thing plainly.

## Structure

**The polished paradox.** "You have to prove the app is being used before it is
allowed to exist." Elegant, symmetrical, quotable, and the reason a paragraph
sounds composed rather than said. Real speech states the two halves and moves on:
"They want a history of real use. The app is not in the directory yet."

**The rule of three where two would do.** "A new row in a sheet, a published
article, a closed deal." Check whether the third item carries information or
rhythm. If rhythm, cut it.

**The closing reversal.** A last line that mirrors the first with the terms
swapped. It feels like landing the plane. It is a tic, and it is visible when
several posts in a row do it.

**Paired slogans.** "Keep the launch, drop the expectation." Two clauses balanced
against each other, neither of them a fact. Break into ordinary sentences.

**Withheld information as a hook.** "With a Beta tag, which I will get to."
Anything of the form "more on that below" inside a short post. Say it where it
belongs.

## Sentences

**Uniform length.** Six sentences of eighteen words each. Nothing else marks a
draft as generated so reliably. Vary deliberately: a four-word sentence after a
long one does more than any word choice.

**Empty intensifiers.** *literally, genuinely, actually, truly, simply, really,
incredibly.* Almost always deletable with no loss.

**Hedging stacks.** "It might be worth considering whether it could perhaps be
useful." One hedge is honest. Three is padding.

**Metaphor standing in for a fact.** "A door with a person behind it." Say what
happened instead.

## Content

**A claim with no owner.** Percentages, "studies show", "most teams". If the
number cannot be attributed, cut it.

**Insight with no cost.** A lesson that did not cost the writer anything reads as
advice from nobody. The angle libraries in `angles.md` all ask for a specific
fact for this reason: the price is the credibility.

**Lines reused this week.** A phrase that appeared in another post, or in the
article this post is built on. Fresh to the writer, repetitive to the reader who
follows both.

**Register aimed at the wrong room.** "So the rest of your stack can react to
it" in a feed of marketers. Jargon is not a tell by itself; jargon in front of
people who do not use it is.

## Formatting

**Bold scattered through prose** to mark importance the sentences do not earn.

**Emoji or hashtags the user does not use.** Match their existing count, which
is often zero. See `voice.md`.

**Markdown in places that do not render it.** Asterisks and bullet characters
show up literally in most social composers and in email. Write plain text.

## The final read

Read the draft out loud, all of it. Anything you would not say to a person in a
conversation gets rewritten or cut. This one test catches more than the entire
list above, and it is the one most often skipped.
