# Openings

The angle decides what the post is about. The opening decides whether anyone
reads past it. They are separate problems and this file is only about the second.

## The constraint

On LinkedIn the feed shows roughly the first 140–210 characters on mobile and
somewhat more on desktop before it collapses the rest behind "see more". The
exact number moves and is not worth chasing. What matters is the consequence:
**the first two lines are the whole post to most of the people who scroll past
it.** Everything else is read only by people the opening already convinced.

On X the entire post is the opening. On Threads and Bluesky the first line
carries almost the same weight as on LinkedIn.

## What an opening has to do

One job: make the next line necessary. Not intriguing, not clever — necessary.
A reader continues when the first line leaves something open that they want
closed.

Three things reliably leave something open:

- **A specific number that does not add up.** "Eight rounds of review for a
  three-line integration." The reader wants the explanation.
- **A stated position that could be argued with.** "I would not follow that
  advice again." The reader wants to know why not.
- **A concrete moment mid-action.** "The post went out at 9am. The wrong
  account." The reader wants the rest of the scene.

## What does not work

- **A summary.** "Here are three lessons from launching our integration." The
  reader now knows what the post is and does not need it.
- **A greeting or a wind-up.** "I have been thinking a lot lately about…" Two
  lines spent arriving at the topic are two lines the reader will not spend.
- **A rhetorical question the reader can answer with "no".** "Ever wondered how
  hard it is to publish an app?" No.
- **Withheld information as bait.** "There is one thing I did not expect, and
  I will get to it." Say the thing. Teasing inside a 200-character window wastes
  the window on the tease.
- **A statistic with no owner.** "73% of marketers say…" The reader has seen
  four of these today and believes none of them.

## Building one from the core

Every angle in `angles.md` names a **core**: the fact that has to come from the
user. The opening is almost always that core, stated plainly and early, with the
context removed.

The user says: *"We announced the Zapier integration and it took eight rounds of
reviewer notes before they let it in, which I didn't expect because the app only
does three things."*

- Weak: "I want to share what I learned publishing our app on Zapier."
- Weak: "Publishing an app is harder than it looks. Here's what happened."
- Strong: "Eight rounds of reviewer notes. The app does three things."

The strong version is shorter than both weak ones. That is usual.

## Test before showing

Read only the first two lines aloud and ask what a stranger now wants to know.
If the answer is "nothing", the opening is a summary and the post starts in the
wrong place. Move the most specific sentence in the draft to the top and reread.
