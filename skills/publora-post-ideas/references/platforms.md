# One angle, several networks

The angle does not change per network. The shape does, and pasting one text
everywhere is the most common way a good angle lands badly.

Ask which network before drafting, or read `list_connections`. If the answer is
several, write per-network versions rather than one compromise.

## LinkedIn

Up to 3000 characters; the feed collapses everything after roughly the first two
lines, so the opening carries the post (see `hooks.md`).

Shape: a full arc. Situation, the turn, what changed. Three to eight short
paragraphs with blank lines between them, because a wall of text is not read on
a phone. First person. A closing line that is a statement or a real question,
not a call to action.

Links cost reach and, more importantly, split attention. One link, pointing at
whatever the post actually asks the reader to do. Depth goes in a comment.

## X

280 characters. The entire post is the opening, so the angle has to survive
compression to its single sharpest line. If it does not survive, it is a
LinkedIn angle and should stay there.

Do not turn a long post into a thread by cutting it into pieces. A thread works
when each part earns the next; a sliced essay does not.

## Threads and Bluesky

500 characters on Threads, similar on Bluesky. Conversational, unfinished,
lower stakes than LinkedIn. An open ending works better than a conclusion —
these feeds reward a reply, not a nod.

Bluesky tolerates more specificity and more strangeness. The same angle can be
weirder there and lose nothing.

## Facebook and Telegram

LinkedIn's arc without the professional register. Longer paragraphs are fine.
Telegram in particular reads as a message rather than a post, so the opening
does not have to fight for attention the same way.

## Instagram, TikTok, YouTube

These will not publish without media, and that changes the order of work: decide
what the image or clip shows **before** drafting a word, because the caption
supports the visual rather than the other way round.

The angle becomes the caption. Instagram allows 2200 characters but the feed
shows about two lines; TikTok captions are read after the video has already
worked. If the angle has no visual, it is not an angle for these networks.

## Choosing rather than covering

The strongest move is usually one network, well. An angle built on a specific
professional failure belongs on LinkedIn and nowhere else. Offer the spread only
when the angle genuinely survives it, and say plainly which network it was shaped
for when handing over the draft.
